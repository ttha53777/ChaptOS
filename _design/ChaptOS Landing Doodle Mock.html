Treasury & dues

Stop being the person who asks for money.

"Someone Venmo'd $70 with a pizza emoji. I have no idea who it was, and dues are $140, so is that half, or is that a different thing entirely?" — every treasurer, week three

ChaptOS keeps one ledger per member per term: what's owed, what landed, what's on a plan. Payments come in labeled. Reminders go out on a schedule you set — and never to someone who already paid.

  • Per-member balances that survive officer turnover
  • Payment plans, waivers, and late rules as actual settings
  • A budget report your advisor accepts without a screenshot
The pizza emoji problem, solved. Payments arrive attached to a person, not a memory.
Attendance

Roll call that takes 40 seconds, not 10 minutes.

"Our attendance policy says three misses and you're out. Nobody can prove who's at three, so the policy has never once been enforced." — secretary, 90-member org

Open the meeting, tap names as people walk in, or let members check in from their phone. Excused absences are requests, not arguments. Standing totals are always current — including the ones nobody wants to bring up.

  • Works for meetings, service hours, practices, and shifts
  • Excuse requests with a paper trail, approved in one tap
  • "Who's at risk of falling below standing?" — answered before the vote
The notebook is not in a car. It's in the meeting, and it counts itself.
Events & timeline

Nobody has to ask what's happening this week.

"We posted the formal three times — group chat, story, email. People still showed up an hour late, and nobody knew who was bringing the van." — social chair, four group chats deep

One term timeline the whole org actually opens. Everybody sees what's coming; each person also sees the piece that's on them — the setup shift, the form, the ride they said they'd drive. You post it once, and it's on everyone's week.

  • One calendar the whole org sees — not four chats and a flyer
  • Every event carries its own tasks, owners, and RSVPs
  • Everyone gets their own short list — and a nudge before it's due
One week, 52 copies. Everybody sees the whole thing — and the part that's theirs.
Roster, roles & handoff

The org shouldn't reset every May.

"Everything our last president knew — the vendor, the form, the deadline, the password — left with her. We rebuilt it from scratch. Badly." — incoming exec board, spring transition

Roles carry permissions, not tribal knowledge. When officers change, access moves with the title — and next year's board inherits the roster, the records, the vendors, and every decision that got logged along the way.

  • Officer transition as a checklist, not a group chat
  • Granular permissions — treasurers see money, chairs see their committee
  • Ask about last year: "what did we spend on formal in 2025?"
Year four still works. The org keeps its memory even when everyone graduates.
