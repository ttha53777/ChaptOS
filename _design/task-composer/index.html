Figurints · ChaptOS — new-task modal redesign

Filing a task should show you where it lands.

The board groups open tasks by computed urgency and paints each row's spine to match — but the form that creates them is a bare date field that never mentions it. This composer is the row, drafted: the title is written in the serif it'll be read in, every date choice carries the band it falls into, assignees have faces and a headcount, and the finished row is visible under your hands the whole time. Both columns are live — type, pick a date, toggle people.

A · Banded quick rail — named days, exact date one click away
B · Two-week strip — the gradient of consequence is the control

What each change is buying

The date says its band

lib/tasks/urgency.ts already decides everything: under three days is Urgent (rose spine), inside a week is Due soon (gold), past that is quiet. Today's form hides that behind <input type="date">. Here the dots and the sentence under the control state it before you commit — and the preview row's spine changes color as you pick.

Faces, a filter, and selected-first

The current picker is a wall of identical text chips in roster order, so at forty members you lose your own selections. Initials give each chip a shape to aim at, the filter handles long rosters, picks float to the front, and the count is always on screen.

A role is a headcount

“Roles expand to their current holders” is a footnote today. Here each role carries its holder count and the pane totals the resolved headcount — assigning Rush Committee tells you you just handed work to six people.

Notes stop paying rent

An always-open empty textarea costs every task author vertical space for a field most skip. One line — Add a note — until it's wanted, matching the poll composer's “Add an option”.

The preview is the row

Markup and tokens are lifted from .tk-row, so the preview isn't an approximation — spine, circle, mono due-chip, avatar chips and the italic note snippet are the same grammar the board uses.

Nothing new was invented

Same five inputs the API takes today (title, optional due date, individuals/roles/everyone, notes) and the same dusk palette. No new fields, no new endpoints — the difference is entirely in what the form tells you while you fill it.
