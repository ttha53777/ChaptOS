Chapter Sync · v4 · loading state

The Late Member

Two runners cross a courtyard to make the meeting. They are not a metaphor for the sync — they are the two requests, and every step is caused by something that actually happened on the wire.

The lineage rule from v2 still governs: every element on the loading screen must be about data we are actually reading. A cartoon is the most likely thing to break it, because a cartoon wants to be about itself — and a cartoon that costs three seconds while telling you nothing is charming once and infuriating by view forty.

So Runner A is GET /api/brothers and Runner B is GET /api/treasury. B enters late and from above because treasury fires second — the stairwell is the request waterfall drawn. The cargo stamps the real count when the response lands. A runner surging ahead means his promise resolved; a runner bent over at the door means his source finished first and he is waiting on the other one, which is a thing a spinner physically cannot tell you.

There is no door on screen during the run. That is the one deliberate deviation from the script: a framed door at a fixed distance is a progress bar, and one that lies, since we do not know the remaining duration. The door is placed the moment the last promise resolves. It costs about a second more than the script's 0.4s arrival — the door has to travel — and it keeps the frame from claiming anything it does not know.

Beat sheet, and what actually fires each beat

The middle column is the point. If a beat has no cause in that column, it is decoration and it gets cut.

BeatLengthWhat you see · what fires it
1 · Entry0 – 0.8s Runner A jogs in from the left, unhurried. Fixed. Establishes the space and the pace so that any later change of pace reads as meaningful. Starts on fetch() dispatch, not on paint.
2 · Vertical0.8 – 1.6s Runner B bursts down the exterior stairwell, two at a time. The one non-horizontal beat in the film. He is late and above because treasury is dispatched after roster — the stairwell is the waterfall. Fixed length: the real gap is ~40ms, which is not animatable, so this beat is honest about order and stylised about duration. That is the one stylisation in the file and it is flagged here rather than buried.
3 · Near miss1.6 – 2.2s B hops a page A dropped, without breaking stride. One interaction beat, not a pile-up. Fires on collision — when the dropped page's screen x crosses B's — not on a timestamp, so it stays in sync at any scroll speed.
4 · Approach2.2s → resolve Elastic. World scrolls, no door in frame, no claim about distance remaining. The only events here are real: a promise resolving stamps that runner's cargo with its count and surges him forward. At +4.5s one fatigue beat fires — a slump and a shortened stride — paired with a line of text naming the slow source, because by then the user wants a reason more than a joke.
5 · Arrival~1.4s Door scrolls in, both hit it, it shuts, cut to dashboard. Fires on the last promise settling, never on a timer. Runs long against the script's 0.4s — see the note below.

The three fallbacks

Required, not optional — so they are running builds on the rail above, not paragraphs. A fallback you cannot click is a fallback nobody has checked.

Reduced motion

Not the animated scene with the animations stripped — that leaves a frozen cartoon and a spinner, which reads as a broken page. It is the same instrument with the theatre removed: the courtyard drops to a dimmed still, and the two sources become rows that fill in with the same counts, plus a determinate bar.

Every fact the animated cut carries survives here. That is the test for whether the animation was ever carrying information: if the still version has to lose something, the animation was the only place it lived, and that was a bug.

Near-instant (<300ms)

Skips to a short Beat 5 — door already three-quarters shut, ~260ms, cut. The 2.2s intro never plays.

Worth saying plainly: under about 150ms the right answer is no loading screen at all. A flash of courtyard on a cached load is a worse artifact than a blank beat. The door-already-closing cut earns its place in the 150–300ms band and nowhere below it.

Failure

The failing runner drops his box and stops where he stands. So does the other one — there is no door to reach any more, and legs still cycling behind an error card is the product pretending it is still working. The card names which source failed and what is consequently blank; a generic "something went wrong" over a frozen cartoon is the worst of both.

The runners never loop forever. If a request is still open at the timeout, that is a failure and it is drawn as one.

What I changed from the script, and why

Four calls. Three are cuts.

  • changed
    The door is off-screen during Beat 4. The script puts the approach on a visible door and gives Beat 5 0.4s. But a door at a fixed distance is a progress bar, and since we do not know the remaining duration it is a progress bar that lies. Off-screen door costs ~1s more at arrival — it has to travel into frame — and buys a frame that never claims anything false. This is the trade; if it is ever reversed, expect "the loading bar is fake" within a week.
  • kept
    One fatigue beat, not an escalating series. Exactly as written. But it is paired with text naming the slow source — at five seconds the charm has run out and the user wants a reason, and a slump alone is the product doing a bit while it fails to answer.
  • changed
    Jointed SVG instead of sprite sheets. No binaries in a double-click mock; the cycle interpolates at any duration, so the fatigue variant is a --gait/--stride change rather than a second sheet; cargo recolors per source for free.
  • cut
    Alternate skins, and sound. The script already marks skins as post-v1. Sound is out of scope for a static mock — and if it ships, the door thud is the only cue that earns a channel, off by default.
  • kept
    Two runners is the cap. Two because two requests block the dashboard's critical path today, not because two is a nice number. If a third ever blocks, the honest move is a third runner. If a fourth appears, this direction is wrong and v3's group chat scales better — worth knowing the exit condition in advance.

Before this ships

The repeat-view problem

This is the risk the whole direction carries. Ninety identical runs of the same 2.2s intro is exactly the "personality tax" the script names, and it is charged every single session.

Two mitigations worth testing before committing: decay the intro — full film on first load of a session, straight to Beat 4 after that; and never play it on navigation, only on cold entry. If the answer is that it must play every time to be worth building, it is not worth building.

The counts must be real

The cargo numbers here are plausible-not-real. Stamping a placeholder on the cargo is the failure mode that kills this: a number on screen that is not the chapter's number turns the whole frame into a screensaver.

If a count is not available at stamp time, the cargo stays blank. That is the fallback, not a rounded guess.

Frame budget

A loading screen that drops frames is worse than no loading screen, because it is the first thing the user sees and it is stuttering. Everything animating here is transform and opacity only, on a fixed node count with pooled dust and paper — no per-frame DOM churn.

Test on the slowest device in the building before this goes near a release, since the load it plays over is exactly when the main thread is busiest.
