What changes from today

  1. A full-height sheet, not a centered card. Today it's a 732px card squeezed to 92vh inside a padded overlay, with a scrim you can barely hit to close. Here it fills the screen, with a close chevron and pull-down on the header.
  2. Sized to what you can actually see. The sheet's height follows visualViewport, not vh. When the keyboard opens the composer rides on top of it and the thread shrinks above. Today the card stays centred in a viewport that is half covered, so the composer sits under the keyboard and Safari scrolls the whole page to reach it.
  3. 16px in every field. The composer is 14.5px today, so iOS zooms the page in on focus and leaves it zoomed. The composer and the writ's edit inputs are now 16px.
  4. The page behind stays put. The body is locked while the sheet is open, and scrolling stops at the edge of the thread instead of carrying through to the dashboard.
  5. The thread stays pinned to the latest message. If you were reading the bottom when the keyboard opened, you still are afterwards. A ledger step or answer arriving while you've scrolled up won't yank you back down.
  6. A landing worth opening. The greeting uses your name, the ask gets a highlighter, the lede sits on a taped note, and the three starters are ink tickets in the thumb zone. When the keyboard is up, the note drops out and the tickets fold into a sideways strip right above the composer.
  7. Written for touch, not a keyboard. No ⌘K hint, no arrow-key row selection, no "Click any underlined value" hint. Editable writ values carry a pencil. Approve takes the full width at 44px. Follow-ups scroll sideways in one line. Return sends.
  8. A peek opens like a new screen. It slides in over the thread, which keeps its scroll position underneath, and Back returns you there.
Every block maps to a shipped component: ReasoningLedger, AnswerBlock (rows, Sources, Helpful?, follow-ups, ask-back), the tally and week shapes, WritCard (edit, gate, stamp), PeekSheet, ApprovalsView. The data is invented. On a real phone this file runs full-screen with the real keyboard, so you can test it in Safari directly. Add ?frame to force the phone frame.
