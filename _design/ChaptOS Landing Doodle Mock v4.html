What's actually in it

Four pages to start.
Seven more when you need them.

A 12-person committee and a 200-member chapter run the same pages — just fewer of them. Turning one on mid-term doesn't cost you the history.

Money

One ledger per member, one for the org, wired so they can't disagree. Payments and payouts are requests until a treasurer approves them.

People

Attendance, dues, service hours and standing — per member, per term, against the bands you set. Roles decide who sees what.

The week

Ideas become events with an owner and a budget. Confirming publishes to one calendar the whole org opens — and the part that's yours.

The record

Bylaws, forms, vendors and minutes in one searchable library. Terms archive instead of vanishing, so last year is still readable.

Plus tasks, polls, service hours, party books, a social calendar, role permissions, several orgs on one login, and a phone app that isn't a squeezed dashboard. See every page

Why officers hand it the keys

An assistant holding your org's money should be boring about it.

ChaptOS proposes; officers decide. Every number traces back to a record, every action lands in a log, and nothing writes without a human who's allowed to.

It can't act alone

Anything touching money or members arrives as a proposal with an Approve button. If your role can't do it, the card says who can.
