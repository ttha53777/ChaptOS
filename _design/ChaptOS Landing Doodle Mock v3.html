Treasury, dues & budget

Two sets of books that can't disagree.

"Someone Venmo'd $70 with a pizza emoji. Dues are $140. Is that half, or a different thing entirely? And did I ever write down the $84 I paid back?" — every treasurer, week three

One ledger per member per term, and one ledger for the org — wired together so they move at the same time. A dues payment or a reimbursement is a request until a treasurer approves it; approval mints the matching row and adjusts the balance in a single write. Nothing gets hard-deleted.

  • Per-member balances, payment plans, waivers and late rules as real settings
  • Semester budget with allocations, category reports, and trend charts
  • CSV export your advisor accepts without a screenshot
The pizza-emoji problem, solved. Money arrives attached to a person, not to somebody's memory.
Roster, standing & roles

Everyone's whole story, one row each.

"Three misses and you're out — that's the policy. Nobody can prove who's at three, so the policy has never once been enforced." — secretary, 90-member org

Attendance, dues, service hours, GPA and anything else you decide to track, per member, per term — with the bands you set for on-track, watch, and at-risk. Open anyone and you get their history, not a number somebody typed.

  • Your own member fields and your own metrics — jersey number, section, major, anything
  • Roles carry fourteen permission switches and a rank, so nobody can promote themselves
  • Excuses and exemptions are requests with a paper trail, approved in one tap
Standing stops being a rumor. The policy finally has a number behind it.
Timeline, programming & meetings

From "someone should do this" to on the calendar.

"We posted the formal three times — group chat, story, email. People still showed up an hour late, and nobody knew who was bringing the van." — social chair, four group chats deep

Ideas live on a board and move through planning to confirmed, carrying their own prep checklist, owner and budget. Confirming publishes to one org timeline — categories you named and colored yourself — and every member's week shows the part that's theirs.

  • One calendar the whole org opens — not four chats and a flyer
  • Meetings keep their agenda, minutes and attendance in the same place
  • Free-form notes summarize into decisions, action items and what was discussed
One week, 52 copies. Everybody sees the whole thing — and the part that's theirs.
Docs, activity & terms

The org shouldn't reset every May.

"Everything our last president knew — the vendor, the form, the deadline, the login — left with her. We rebuilt it from scratch. Badly." — incoming exec board, spring transition

Bylaws, forms, vendor links and minutes live in a foldered library anyone can search. Every change writes an entry with who and when. Terms archive instead of vanishing — last year is still readable while this year starts clean.

  • Drag-and-drop filing, pinned essentials, link previews, contributor credit
  • An activity log the whole board can read — no "who changed this?"
  • Ask about last year: "what did we spend on formal in 2025?"
Year four still works. The org keeps its memory even when everyone graduates.
