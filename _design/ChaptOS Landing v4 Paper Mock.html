One place instead

The boring parts, handled.

Not a reinvention of how your chapter runs. The same four things you already do every week — in one place that remembers what happened last week.

Stop reconciling Venmo at midnight.

Every payment lands in one ledger — Venmo, Zelle, cash at chapter, the guy who paid his roommate back. Mark it once and the roster, the reminders and the treasurer’s report all update themselves.

  • Venmo notes matched to the right member automatically
  • Reminders go to the 11 people who owe — not @everyone
  • Payment plans, partial payments and waivers without a side sheet
  • A balance sheet your chapter advisor will actually accept

Take attendance in the ninety seconds before chapter starts.

Tap down the roster, or throw a QR code on the projector and let people check themselves in. Excused absences keep their reason attached — so when someone contests a fine in April, you have the record instead of a memory.

  • Excuse requests come in before the meeting, not after the fine
  • Attendance rules you set once — fines calculate themselves
  • Works for chapter, philanthropy, rush, practice — anything on the calendar

The rest of it

And the twenty smaller things.

Nothing here will change your life. Together they’re most of what an e-board actually spends its week doing.

One calendar

Chapter, rush, philanthropy, formal. Everyone sees the same dates, including the ones that moved.

Docs that stay put

Bylaws, budgets, risk plans. Owned by the chapter, not by whoever made the file.

Reports for nationals

Headcount, GPA standing, service totals. Export the shape they ask for instead of rebuilding it.

Standards, quietly

Announcements that land

Send to the 11 who owe, the 9 behind on hours, or everyone. It knows who’s who.
